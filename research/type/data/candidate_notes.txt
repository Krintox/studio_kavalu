## TASK 5a — Real handwriting vs handwriting fonts
[5a] The OpenType spec itself frames the problem: the 'rand' (Randomize) feature exists "In order to emulate the irregularity and variety of handwritten text"; the application picks an alternate "by a pseudo-random algorithm" or by stepping through a stored sequence — https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/opentype/spec/features_pt
[5a] Browser support for 'rand' was not verified here (unverified; my understanding is that browsers do not randomise). What browsers do apply by default is 'calt': MDN says font-variant-ligatures "normal" (the default) "usually activates" contextual alternates (calt) — https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-variant-ligatures
[5a] Glyphs tutorial (Rainer Erich Scheichelbauer, 2012, updated 2022) objects to "a font imitating handwriting but showing two identical glyphs when you type a double letter" and shows calt cycling that "goes 1-2-3-1-2-3", i.e. the variation is a fixed, predictable rotation — https://glyphsapp.com/learn/features-part-3-advanced-contextual-alternates
[5a] Calligraphr: randomisation is "an attempt to simulate true handwriting"; free accounts get up to 2 variants per character, Pro up to 15; ligatures are a Pro feature; export is TTF/OTF — https://www.calligraphr.com/en/docs/faq/
[5a] Calligraphr recommends "at least three to four variants" per character for "an authentic looking handwriting font", more for frequent letters like a and e — https://www.calligraphr.com/en/docs/tutorial2/
[5a] Calligraphr licence for fonts made from your own hand: "Any creation you make with Calligraphr is your sole property", and you are "not required to give any credit to Calligraphr"; the FAQ does not separately address selling or web use (no restriction stated) — https://www.calligraphr.com/en/docs/faq/
[5a] Calligraphr's site lists no separate terms-of-service page in its navigation (only FAQ, pricing, privacy), so the ownership statement above is the operative public text — https://www.calligraphr.com/
[5a] Digitising real handwriting as SVG — Inkscape's Trace Bitmap (Path > Trace Bitmap) turns a scan into vector paths; "Brightness cutoff" makes filled silhouettes; "Centerline tracing (autotrace)" reduces a shape to an open path and is described as best for handwriting and line drawings — https://inkscape-manuals.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tracing-an-image.html
[5a] Potrace (GPL) is the standard bitmap-to-smooth-vector tracer, with SVG output — https://potrace.sourceforge.net/
[5a] Drawn-on handwriting animation uses stroke-dasharray/stroke-dashoffset, and "The shape must have a stroke" (Chris Coyier, CSS-Tricks, 2014) — so notes meant to "write themselves" need centreline (stroked) paths, while filled traced outlines can only fade/mask in — https://css-tricks.com/svg-line-animation-works/
[5a] This project already treats the wordmark this way: "The wordmark is not a font: it is Ananya's own lettering, redrawn as SVG paths" (local file, not a URL) — C:\shashank\shashank\fnf\ananya_residency\studio_kavalu\website\src\fonts\LICENSE-fonts.md
[5a] MY JUDGEMENT: for a handful of founder notes per page, scanned and traced SVGs of her real handwriting beat any font (no repeats, true pressure and baseline drift, no licence question); a Calligraphr font of her hand is the fallback only for notes that must be editable text, built with 3+ variants per frequent letter, and even then the calt cycle will show on longer lines.
