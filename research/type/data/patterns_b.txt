# a2b_patterns.txt — observations per category, each naming the rows in a2b_industries.tsv it rests on
# Collected 2026-10-24. "Verified" rows only unless marked. Roles (display vs text) on Typewolf rows are inferred
# from the fonts' names and design purpose, because Typewolf lists fonts without roles.

## 7. Tech, SaaS and AI
1. AI companies' signature is a bookish serif display over a grotesque text face: Claude (Galaxie Copernicus + Tiempos Text + Styrene), OpenAI 2023 (Signifier + Söhne), Kin (Tobias + Söhne + Söhne Mono), Delphi (Martina Plantijn + Inter), Gradient (New Spirit + Outfit). The serif signals "human, thoughtful". Anthropic's own frontend-design skill now lists "a high-contrast serif display" on cream as a tell of generated pages (a2b_ai_defaults.txt 2C), so this look is worn out for a new brand.
2. Developer and infrastructure SaaS keeps to one grotesque family, often its own: Stripe (Söhne), Linear (Inter Display + Inter), Vercel (Geist Sans + Geist Mono), Retool (Saans + Px Grotesk). A mono cut is the usual "technical" accent (Kin, Vercel).
3. One commercial grotesque, Klim's Söhne, runs through the 2020–25 premium-tech look (Stripe, OpenAI, Kin). Inter and Geist mark the free default end (Linear, Delphi, Vercel). A site set in Söhne-alike or Inter with a serif headline reads as "tech startup" whatever it sells.
4. Vercel open-sourced Geist and Next.js then made it the starter default (a2b_ai_defaults.txt 2A). That is how a house font turns into a commodity: what was distinctive for Vercel is now the v0/shadcn default.
5. Indian tech either licenses a characterful display face over Inter (Razorpay: TASA Orbiter headings + Inter text in its Blade design system) or owns a custom face (Zoho Puvi, named from the Tamil word for Earth). CRED's Cirka headings are partial evidence only. None of the verified Indian tech examples publicly ships Indic scripts in its brand type (Razorpay's tokens are Latin-only; Puvi's script coverage is not stated). Zerodha: unverified.

## 8. Finance and legal
1. Large institutions commission a custom sans for scale and data, then add a classical serif for gravitas: Goldman Sachs (Goldman Sans by Dalton Maag + Sabon), Robinhood (RH Phonic by Schick Toikka + Martina Plantijn headlines), Wise (Wise Sans by NaN, 342 languages + Inter). Navi (Navi Sans, Bengaluru) follows the custom-sans half of this.
2. Neobanks run one family across web, app and billboards: Revolut (Aeonik Pro everywhere), Wise (Wise Sans display, Inter UI).
3. Wealth and private banking keeps high-contrast serifs: Citi Private Bank website (Sainte Colombe + Interstate). Robinhood used a serif to move upmarket (Martina Plantijn for headlines, Porto Rocha 2024).
4. Fintech startup sites copy the AI-site formula: serif headline, grotesque text, mono for figures. Vesto: Tiempos Headline + Lausanne. Origin: Office Times + Haffer + Roboto Mono. This look is widespread now and reads as "Silicon Valley".
5. Law firms on Fonts In Use pair a contemporary serif with a Swiss grotesque: Haavind, Oslo (STK Bureau Serif + ABC Diatype); Cross Border Legalis (Wremena + Founders Grotesk). Big-law rebrands (Clifford Chance, Freshfields, and in India Khaitan) are covered for logos and colours, not typefaces. That is an evidence gap, so don't claim what big law uses.
6. India: AMU Leasing is the only verified Indian finance example that plans for Indian scripts. It uses Anek for vernacular "all scripts" beside Latin Komet headings and Arpona Sans body. SBI's wordmark is a customised geometric sans (unnamed, partial). Jupiter's app fonts are unverified.

## 9. Kids and education
1. Consumer learning brands for children use a chunky, rounded display face with a rounded UI sans: Duolingo (Feather Bold by Fontsmith + DIN Next Rounded), LEGO Duplo (Tobi). Roundness is the category's shorthand. The distinctive part is that Duolingo's display face is custom.
2. Learning platforms choose plain humanist sans and plan for many scripts: Khan Academy (Lato with Noto Sans fallback, Noto Serif, Inconsolata) and StoryWeaver by Pratham Books (Raleway + Bree Serif, with Noto Sans fallbacks for Devanagari, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Gujarati, Gurmukhi, Oriya, Sinhala, Tibetan and Ol Chiki).
3. Universities use open-source superfamily pairs so every department can install them free: Stanford (Source Sans 3 + Source Serif 4, Roboto as accent).
4. Children's arts and advocacy sites set an expressive wide display face over a sober text face: Baboró festival (Obviously + Halyard), Lobby für Kinder (Fictional + Poppins). Moxie (Merriweather + Open Sans) shows the generic end: two stock Google fonts with no voice.
5. India: in StoryWeaver, Indian-language text falls back to Noto while Latin is Raleway, so the two scripts speak in different voices. A bilingual kids' brand should pick a Latin face with a designed Indic sibling (Anek, Mukta, Baloo; see category 12). BYJU'S and Unacademy: unverified.

## 10. Hospitality and travel
1. Boutique hotels and stays pair a soft, calligraphic or high-contrast serif display with a quiet sans: Narai Hospitality Group (Nib + IBM Plex Sans), Emerald Stay (Louize + Acumin), Hotel Europejski Warsaw (IvyMode + Aktiv Grotesk), Versus Hotels (Editorial New + Space Grotesk).
2. Several of those text faces (IBM Plex Sans, Space Grotesk) are on Impeccable's training-data reject list (a2b_ai_defaults.txt 2B). Real sites use them, but as defaults they are a risk. Narai and Versus get away with it because the display face carries the voice.
3. Resorts with an outdoor or leisure voice swap the serif for a friendly geometric sans plus mono details: Jacob's Cove (GT Walsheim + GT Flexa Mono).
4. Big travel brands build custom families for scale and scripts: Korean Air Sans (Dalton Maag Latin, 790+ languages, Hangul by SandollTium, so two foundries feed one system), Air India Sans (FutureBrand 2023, script coverage not stated), Airbnb (LL Circular in 2014, later replaced by its own Cereal, not verified here).
5. Indian luxury hospitality combines hand-lettering with a multi-script house sans: The Leela has an ITF-lettered mark (ornamented script L over engraved-style serif capitals) and ITF's Kohinoor as corporate typeface. ITF also sells Kohinoor Kannada (listed on the ITF site).
6. Indian café and food brands go warm and rounded with Indian-foundry Latin faces: Barista Coffee (Baloo Latin primary, Mukta Latin on the website, both Ek Type companions of Indic families) and Biergarten Bengaluru (Bungee + Anek Kannada). Taj and The Postcard: unverified.

## 11. NGOs and non-profits
1. Big digital non-profits choose reach and speed over flourish: Wikipedia uses no webfonts at all (device system sans; Linux Libertine/Georgia for headings, per Wikimedia Codex tokens). Mozilla's brand sites use its own Mozilla Headline and Mozilla Text; Firefox sites use Metropolis + Inter.
2. Charity identity refreshes usually take one characterful grotesque, or a warm serif with a mono: One for the World (Forma DJR), WWF Market (NN Nouvelle Grotesk), Ayuda en Acción (Bogart for warmth + DM Mono for "digital, professional").
3. Non-profit text faces are often stock (Open Sans at Population Foundation of India, DM Mono at Ayuda en Acción, Poppins at Lobby für Kinder). The distinctiveness has to come from the display face (Sharp Grotesk, Bogart) or from illustration.
4. India: Population Foundation of India (Lopez Design: Sharp Grotesk + Open Sans) credits only Latin type, although it works in Hindi-speaking states. Pratham Books/StoryWeaver handles ten Indian scripts through Noto fallbacks. Goonj and Akshaya Patra: unverified (no Fonts In Use entries, no other source reached).

## 12. Indian and bilingual brands (Latin with Devanagari or Kannada)
1. Route A, one superfamily drawn across scripts. Ek Type's Mukta covers Devanagari, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Tamil and Latin and is used by Trolley Times (Hindi + Punjabi + Latin), the Maharashtra Covid campaign and Nagpur Metro. Ek Type's Anek covers ten scripts, Kannada and Latin among them, and is used by Biergarten Bengaluru (Anek Kannada), the IPL (Anek Telugu) and AMU Leasing. This is the cheapest way to make the scripts look like one family.
2. Route B, a bespoke Indic face matched to an existing Latin. Starbucks India: ITF drew a Devanagari logotype with Landor Paris and tuned terminals, conjunct joins and letter height to the Latin's visual weight. Nirmala UI: Tiro drew 14 Indic scripts coordinated with Segoe, legible at 8–9 pt. GT America Devanagari: Shiva Nallaperumal and Hitesh Malaviya aimed for "a Devanagari equivalent", not a version, matched "in spirit and form". Korean Air (Hangul by SandollTium) is the same route outside India.
3. Matching criteria the sources name: relative height (Hind's Devanagari base characters are 94% as tall as the Latin capitals), visual weight and terminals (Starbucks), form and texture over historical roots (GT Devanagari), and stroke-contrast direction (Prajavani Headline reverses traditional Kannada contrast so it can be condensed).
4. The deepest Indian-script type work is in news and broadcast, not consumer tech: Prajavani, Bengaluru (Kannada, ITF), Lokmat (Sama Devanagari, Ek Type), Anandabazar Patrika (Bangla, Tiro), Star Jalsa and Life OK (custom Bengali and Devanagari, Ek Type).
5. Many big Indian brands publish Latin-only type: Air India Sans (Indic coverage not stated), Mumbai airport's PF Centro Sans, Navi Sans, Zoho Puvi. Hindi and Kannada are then handled ad hoc. Swiggy, Zomato, Tanishq, Fabindia, Amul, Namma Metro, BLR Airport and Karnataka Tourism: unverified in this pass.
6. Kannada options seen in the sources: Anek Kannada (Ek Type, width and weight range), Baloo Tamma 2 (Ek Type, Kannada + Latin, warm), Tiro Kannada (John Hudson and Fiona Ross, literary), Noto Sans Kannada (StoryWeaver's fallback), Kohinoor Kannada (ITF), Fit Kannada (Mota Italic), and Prajavani's custom Text and Headline. Moksha Gin (New Zealand) shows a Latin plus Devanagari wordmark lockup using ITF's Pancho Devanagari.
